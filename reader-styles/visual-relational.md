# Reader style: visual-relational

## Who

- **Reader:** someone who asks for structure they can see: relations as graphs, sets as regions, quantities as geometry, kinds as colours, and little text.
- **Expertise:** any level.
- **Reading mode:** scans the figure first, then reads the text that remains.

This is a stated preference, not a learning style. The content advice below is supported for
every reader: diagrams that put related items next to each other save search and inference
(Larkin & Simon 1987), and words with a picture beat words alone (Mayer's multimedia principle).
The preference sets how far to push it.

## Overrides

| Principle | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| [draw relations](../principles/page/draw-relations.md) | strengthen: any relation, set, quantity or process is drawn before it is described; the prose then refers to the figure and does not re-describe it | preference, on top of evidence that holds for all readers ([learning science](../evidence/learning-science/)) |
| [signal the structure](../principles/page/signal-the-structure.md) | strengthen: text is a tree; one idea per line; tables and lists before paragraphs; no block past three lines | preference; the signaling effect itself is measured ([typography and layout](../evidence/typography-and-layout/)) |
| [define before use](../principles/document/define-before-use.md) | strengthen: a symbol is named in the sentence before the formula that uses it | preference, consistent with pre-training (Mayer) |
| (colour) | add: one colour per kind of thing, the same across a whole document | preference, untested; colour must never be the only cue (WCAG 2.2, 1.4.1) |
| (motion) | add: anything that changes over time is shown stepping, at the reader's pace | animation beats static pictures only modestly, depending on what is shown (Höffler & Leutner 2007; Berney & Bétrancourt 2016); letting the reader set the pace helps (Mayer & Chandler 2001) |

## Thresholds

None changed.

## Open questions

Whether drawing everything has a cost for this reader when the relation is trivial; untested.
