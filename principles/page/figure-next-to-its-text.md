# Figure next to its text

**Claim:** the words that explain a figure, a formula or a block of code sit beside or inside it, not on another page or below the fold.

- **Grade:** measured.
- **Evidence:** [learning science](../../evidence/learning-science/review.md) (strand 5), [typography and layout](../../evidence/typography-and-layout/review.md) (strand 6).
- **Check:** `figure-captions`: every image has alt text, and every drawn figure has words right beside it.
- **Reader styles:** none override it.

## What it asks

- Labels on the figure rather than in a legend; the caption is a sentence that says what to see.
- The prose refers to the figure and does not describe it again.
- A formula's symbols are named in the sentence just before it.

## Why

Placing words next to the picture they explain raised learning, d = 0.72, and far more for complex material than for
simple (0.78 against 0.28), in novices only (Ginns 2006, a meta-analysis). A split figure makes the reader hold one part in memory while searching for the other
(split attention; Ayres 2014).

## Limits

The effect is measured on learning material; for reference material read by experts it is untested.
