# Signal the structure

**Claim:** the structure of a text is visible before it is read: headings that name their content, lists for parallel items, tables for comparisons, bold for the defined term.

- **Grade:** measured for signals (headings, labels, cues); tables for comparisons supported with caveat, from
  cognitive fit; lists for parallel items and bold for the defined term are conventions.
- **Evidence:** [learning science](../../evidence/learning-science/review.md) (strand 5), [typography and layout](../../evidence/typography-and-layout/review.md) (strand 5).
- **Check:** `paragraph-length` (heuristic): no paragraph past four sentences, no run past 25 statements under one heading.
- **Reader styles:** strengthened by [visual-relational](../../reader-styles/visual-relational.md) and [LLM reader](../../reader-styles/llm-reader.md).

## What it asks

- Headings say what the section holds, not that a section starts ("Results of the timing test", not "Results").
- Three or more parallel items are a list; two or more items compared on the same attributes are a table.
- Short blocks, with the key word first: readers scan the first lines and the left edge most.
- One way to mark each meaning, kept across the whole document.

## Why

- **Signalling** raises retention, g = 0.53, and transfer, 0.33, over 103 studies (Schneider 2018, measured). The
  cues it pools are organisational signals, colour and labels; the evidence here names no effect for lists, or for
  bold that marks a definition.
- **Prior knowledge:** whether readers with little of it gain most is contested. One meta-analysis says so (Richter
  2016), and Schneider 2018 found no important moderator.
- **Tables** suit looking up exact values, and graphs spatial tasks (Vessey 1991, a review of cognitive fit). That
  supports a table for values compared on the same attributes.
- Lists and bold keywords are practice, argued from Miller 1956 and anecdote (Moran 2016).
- **Scanning:** most web users scan, and a scannable page raised measured usability by 47% on one test site (Nielsen
  1997, company research).

## Limits

Nielsen Norman Group reports that readers often miss content folded behind a toggle (Wang 2023). That source is a
company article below the quality bar: an example, not evidence. Fold only what is optional, or what must stay
hidden until the reader asks for it: the answer to a [retrieval prompt](../document/retrieval-prompts.md), for one.
