# Signal the structure

**Claim:** the structure of a text is visible before it is read: headings that name their content, lists for parallel items, tables for comparisons, bold for the defined term.

- **Grade:** measured.
- **Evidence:** [learning science](../../evidence/learning-science/review.md) (strand 5), [typography and layout](../../evidence/typography-and-layout/review.md) (strand 5).
- **Check:** `paragraph-length` (heuristic): no paragraph past four sentences, no run past 25 statements under one heading.
- **Reader styles:** strengthened by [visual-relational](../../reader-styles/visual-relational.md) and [LLM reader](../../reader-styles/llm-reader.md).

## What it asks

- Headings say what the section holds, not that a section starts ("Results of the timing test", not "Results").
- Three or more parallel items are a list; two or more items compared on the same attributes are a table.
- Short blocks, with the key word first: readers scan the first lines and the left edge most.
- One way to mark each meaning, kept across the whole document.

## Why

Signalling raises retention, g = 0.53, and transfer, 0.33, over 103 studies (Schneider 2018, measured). Whether
readers with low prior knowledge gain most is contested: one meta-analysis says so (Richter 2016), and this one
found no important moderator. Most web users scan, and a scannable page raised
measured usability by 47% on one test site (Nielsen 1997, company research).

## Limits

Nielsen Norman Group reports that readers often miss content folded behind a toggle (Wang 2023). That source is a
company article below the quality bar: an example, not evidence. Fold only what is optional.
