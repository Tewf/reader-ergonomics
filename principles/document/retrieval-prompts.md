# Retrieval prompts

**Claim:** a document meant to be learned ends each part with a few questions the reader answers from memory, with the answers folded, and is reread after a gap.

- **Grade:** measured.
- **Evidence:** [learning science](../../evidence/learning-science/review.md) (strands 1 and 2).
- **Check:** `shape-conformance` (exact, for documents declared as notes in config): the questions are present; whether they test recall is judgment.
- **Reader styles:** none override it; it does not apply to pure reference material.

## What it asks

- Two or three questions per part, answerable without the text; answers hidden until asked for.
- A question placed before a part only for what must be learned: prequestions help the questioned material and nothing else.
- Spaced rereading: the gap grows with how long the material must last.

## Why

Practice testing beats restudy, g = 0.33, and g = 0.50 against all control conditions, over 222 classroom
studies (Yang 2021); it helps more with feedback (Rowland 2014).
Spaced retrieval beats massed, g = 0.74 (Latimier 2021). Prequestions give g = 0.54 on the questioned material and
0.04 on the rest (St. Hilaire 2023).

## Limits

Restudy wins at five minutes; testing wins at two days and a week (Roediger 2006). Whether the gaps expand or stay
equal makes no measurable difference, g = 0.03 (Latimier 2021).
