# State of the art

Upper bounds: what has been achieved. Lower bounds: what is shown not to work or to cost something.

## Upper bounds, per sub-question

| Sub-question | Best result | Method | Measured how |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overview first | graphic organizer g = 0.70 memory, 0.53 comprehension (Ponce 2025; publication bias, so possibly inflated) | meta-analysis | memory and comprehension tests |
| Context timing | topic before the passage helps, after does not (Bransford and Johnson 1972) | randomised experiment | comprehension and recall |
| Goal-set depth | prequestions g = 0.54 on prequestioned items, 0.04 on the rest (St. Hilaire 2023) | meta-analysis | memory tests |
| Order within a unit | example then problem beats problems only (van Gog 2011) | randomised experiment | similar and transfer problems |
| Order of lessons | alternating concepts and procedures beats concepts first (Rittle-Johnson 2015) | review and experiments | procedures and transfer |
| Order across units | whole 4C/ID programmes d = 0.79 (Costa 2022, sequencing not isolated); interleaving g = 0.42, reversed for words (Brunmair 2019) | meta-analyses | performance tests (Costa); transfer and retention (Brunmair) |
| Hypertext order | most-related-next helps low-knowledge readers (Salmerón 2005, 2006) | randomised experiments | comprehension after hypertext reading |
| Proofs | self-explanation training d = 0.95 (Hodds 2014) | randomised experiments | proof comprehension tests |
| Where to stop | a few correct recalls, then returns diminish (Rawson and Dunlosky 2011) | randomised experiments | delayed recall |

## Lower bounds and costs

| Limit | Source | Measured how |
|---|---|---|
| Organizer effect small on immediate tests (d = 0.21, Luiten; delayed 0.19 to 0.38), mean 0.66 (median 0.48) on delayed tests (Stone, read); unfamiliar-material condition supported by Mayer's vote count, not by Stone's effect sizes (tiny k) | Luiten 1980; Stone 1983; Mayer 1979 | meta-analyses; theory with experiments |
| Skimming gives only moderate comprehension | Rayner 2016 | review |
| Goal-bounded depth lowers learning of the non-goal part | Klauer 1984; McCrudden 2005 | meta-analysis; experiment |
| Withholding assistance beat giving it for advanced learners (d = -0.43, CI -0.65 to -0.21, high heterogeneity); the gain for novices is larger (+0.51) | Tetzlaff 2025 | meta-analysis |
| Parts trained apart transfer badly; fixed difficulty steps fail | Wickens 2013 | meta-analysis |
| Felt understanding overstates real understanding | Rozenblit and Keil 2002 | experiments |
| Overconfident learners stop too early | Dunlosky and Rawson 2012 | experiments |

## Holder of the record

- No study holds a record for breadth-first against depth-first reading of the same hierarchical material: none was found.
- The best-supported components are separate: overview first, goal-set depth, self-test as the stop.

## Most adopted

A record of practice, kept apart from the research record above.

| Sub-question | Most adopted | Best evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Conclusion first | BLUF, Pyramid Principle, executive summary | no controlled test of BLUF found |
| Importance order in news | inverted pyramid, the newsroom default | Nildam 1998 favours narrative for Thai news readers, Sternadori 2008 is mixed; both theses |
| Conclusion first for bad news | taught as an exception | explanation first rated slightly more readable in one experiment, with no comprehension difference (Jansen 2010) |
| Web scanning | front-load the key facts (Nielsen 2006) | descriptive eyetracking, n = 232; no learning outcome |
| Labelled structure | structured abstracts | more information, no recall gain at equal length (Hartley 2004) |
