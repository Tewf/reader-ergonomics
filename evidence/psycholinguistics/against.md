# Against

Terms are defined in `review.md`; works are listed in the [bibliography](../bibliography.md).

## 1. Linear or super-linear surprisal cost

- For linear: Smith & Levy 2013; Wilcox et al. 2023; Shain et al. 2024.
- For super-linear: Meister et al. 2021 (weak for reading time); Hoover et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2023 (some languages only).
- For sub-linear in surprisal: Brothers & Kuperberg 2021, whose cost is linear in probability.
- Failed replication: the same items under eye-tracking give a logarithmic effect (Buggy et al. 2026, preprint).
- Source of disagreement 1: the language model that estimates surprisal (Xu et al. 2023; Oh & Schuler 2023).
- Source of disagreement 2: the measure, self-paced reading or eye-tracking.
- Source of disagreement 3: the range sampled; cloze norms cannot resolve small probabilities (Buggy et al. 2026).
- Production is not comprehension: UID evidence from speakers (Jaeger 2010) says nothing direct about reader cost.
- Entropy rate constancy did not hold with neural models (Verma et al. 2023).
- Information rate rises and falls with discourse structure, so uniformity is not the only pressure (Tsipidi et al. 2024).

## 2. Locality: corpus regularity or reader cost

- Shorter dependencies in corpora (Futrell et al. 2015) do not by themselves show a cost to readers.
- In naturalistic reading, integration cost is weak outside nouns (Demberg & Keller 2008).
- The random baseline is disputed: non-crossing may itself result from minimization, so a projective baseline builds in part of the effect (Ferrer-i-Cancho & Gómez-Rodríguez 2015).
- The "optimal" of Futrell et al. 2015 is the projective minimum; the unconstrained minimum is lower or equal.
- A rival mechanism: retrieval interference, not distance, may carry the cost (Lewis & Vasishth 2005).
- Yngve depth: a corpus shows a bias against left-branching but no sharp limit (Sampson 1997).

## 3. Nesting: corpus limits and processing limits

- Karlsson 2007 counts what writers produced; it does not measure what readers can parse.
- Karlsson 2007 ties multiple center-embedding to a written stylistic tradition, so its rarity is partly cultural.
- Processing limits depend on the grammar: the missing-verb illusion holds in English, not in German (Vasishth et al. 2010).
- The illusion appears at degree 2 (Gibson & Thomas 1999), below the corpus maximum of 3.

## 4. Reader dependence

- The reverse cohesion effect depends on skill: skilled high-knowledge readers gained from high cohesion (O'Reilly & McNamara 2007).
- It depends on the measure: situation-model tasks, not recall (McNamara et al. 1996b).
- One of its results did not replicate in a second experiment (McNamara & Kintsch 1996a).
- Expertise reversal: a rule tuned for novices is not neutral for experts (Kalyuga et al. 2003).
- Consequence: no density rule is reader-free; each needs a stated reader.

## 5. From the lab to notes that are reread and searched

- Lab items: sentences or short passages, read once, often word by word.
- Notes: reread, skimmed, searched by heading or keyword.
- Rereading changes eye movements: shorter and fewer fixations, longer saccades (Raney & Rayner 1995).
- Skimming under time pressure favors early parts of paragraphs and pages, and depends on navigation links (Duggan & Payne 2009).
- Competent adults read the same text with distinct strategies; heading users summarize best (Hyönä et al. 2002).
- Grade for each transfer from lab to notes: convention, untested.

## 6. Readability formulas

- Formulas count sentence length and word familiarity; they do not define what makes a text hard (Davison & Kantor 1982).
- Adaptations written to a formula can become harder to understand (Davison & Kantor 1982).
- For adults, NLP-feature models predict comprehension better than classic formulas (Crossley et al. 2017).
- Coh-Metrix indices are correlates of difficulty; a rule that moves an index is not shown to move comprehension.

## 7. Practitioner and descriptive theories

- Gopen & Swan 1990 report no controlled test; parts of it agree with given-new evidence (Haviland & Clark 1974) and locality (Grodner & Gibson 2005).
- Centering's uniqueness constraint holds or fails depending on parameter settings (Poesio et al. 2004).
- RST describes structure; this review found no test that RST depth itself predicts reading cost.

## 8. Practice that contradicts a principle

- Practice: rewrite until a grade tool passes (Vale 2026; Hemingway 2026; textstat 2026).
- Principle: Even information density is not supported as a way to read faster.
- Evidence: adaptations written to a formula can read worse (Davison & Kantor 1982); formulas ignore readers (Redish 2000; Schriver 2017).
- Practice: a Flesch floor fixed by statute (NAIC 2025), against the weak adult baseline in section 6.
- Practice: splitting to a word cap; the EC warns to keep link words so coherence survives (EC 2011).
- Tool caveat: textstat's own README limits SMOG to texts of at least 30 sentences.
