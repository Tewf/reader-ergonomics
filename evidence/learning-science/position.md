# Position

## Known (measured)

- Retrieval and spacing are the best-supported study techniques, and gains grow after a delay.
- Worked examples help novices; guidance should fade as expertise grows.
- Constructing a map beats studying one.
- Words belong beside the picture or code they explain.
- Visual cues (bold, headings, arrows) help, most for low prior knowledge.
- Case comparison transfers better than studying cases one by one, if the reader is prompted.

## Known with caveat

- Skeletal notes: positive, from older randomised experiments.
- Pre-training on key terms: large effect, mostly from one lab.
- Hypertext links: load is reasoned from theory and a few small studies.
- Mastery gating: older meta-analysis, costs time.

## Not known

- No study tests a numeric section-length threshold such as 80 statements.
- No study tests the depth of layering across several linked notes.
- Definition-first versus example-first has no deciding study.
- Atomic notes, maps of content and progressive summarisation have no controlled study.
- Spaced-repetition schedulers are benchmarked on recall prediction, not on exam scores.
- Most effects come from short materials and near transfer.

## What this repo adds

- It restates each field result as a general claim about readers, with its grade.
- It keeps measured claims apart from conventions, so a rule can be traced to its level of evidence.
- It does not claim that no one has tested a rule, only that the review below found no test.
